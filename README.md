# Captain Veggie 🦸🥦

A tiny, kind educational game for children. A small superhero gets **bigger,
stronger, faster, and learns to fly** by eating his veggies, fruits, and grains.

It captures the spirit of *"please eat your spinach, it makes you strong!"* —
playfully, not literally. It is **not** meant to be medically accurate.

## Try it

Just open `index.html` in any browser (desktop or phone). No build, no install.
Tap/click the falling foods.

## The core design problem — and how it's solved

You asked for something subtle and important:

> Eating something unhealthy must **not** "make you sick." The only direction
> should be **up**. But eating too much junk should still *feel* like the
> lesser choice, and eating well should *feel* good.

The trick is to move all the meaning into **reward and opportunity cost**,
never into punishment:

| | Healthy food 🥦🍎🍚🫘💧 | Junk food 🍭🍫🥤 |
|---|---|---|
| Effect | Sparkles, fills the rainbow, grows you, unlocks powers | A harmless little *poof* — "yum… but no power" |
| Health | Only ever goes **up** | **Never** changes. No sickness, ever. |
| Feeling | "I'm getting stronger!" | "That was tasty, but I don't feel any stronger…" |

So junk food is never a trap or a penalty. A child *cannot* hurt their
character by eating chocolate. The chocolate simply doesn't do anything —
and right next to it, the broccoli is fizzing with sparkles and making the
hero grow. **The contrast does the teaching.** Doing nothing, next to
something wonderful, reads clearly as "the boring choice" without ever
being "the bad choice that harms me."

## Variety is the real superpower 🌈

This is the heart of the game and the answer to *"eat a variety of things."*

There are five healthy **food groups**, each a color of a **Power Rainbow**:

- 🥦 Veggies (green)
- 🍎 Fruit (red)
- 🍚 Grains (gold)
- 🫘 Protein / beans (purple)
- 💧 Water / milk (blue)

You **level up only when you complete a full rainbow** — one of every color.
Eating ten broccolis in a row does *not* level you up; it just keeps lighting
the same green dot. To grow you must eat **across all five groups**. The hero
even nudges the player: *"Yum! Now let's try a different color!"*

This makes "eat a variety" a literal, felt game mechanic rather than a slogan.

## What you unlock (always up, never down)

Each completed rainbow grants the next power:

1. **Grow** — the hero gets visibly bigger
2. **Strong** — bigger still
3. **Speed** — wiggling speed-lines ⚡
4. **Flight** — a cape appears and he flies 🦸
5. **Super Captain Veggie** — a golden glow 🌟

Powers only stack upward. There is no decay, no failure state, no game over.

## How it plays

- Foods gently float down the screen. Tap the ones you want to eat.
- Missed food just drifts off the bottom — **no penalty** for missing.
- Pace is slow and forgiving, tuned for young children.
- Big friendly buttons, emoji art (no assets to load), works on touch screens.

## What this is (and isn't)

This is a **prototype** to test the central feel: *can "good is rewarding,
junk is merely unrewarding" carry the message without any punishment?* I think
it does, and it's genuinely fun to chase the rainbow.

## Ideas for where to take it next

- **Themed levels / a goal**: light up a dark city, rescue a kitten in a tree —
  giving the powers a purpose so progress feels earned.
- **Character customization**: pick your hero, unlock cape colors with variety.
- **Gentle "balance" hint, never a punishment**: a smiley "energy" face that
  beams when you've eaten a varied meal — still only ever positive.
- **Parent/co-play mode**: a grown-up names a real food and the matching one
  appears to tap ("what's green and makes you strong?").
- **Sound & music**: happy crunch for healthy, a soft comedic *blop* for junk.
- **Accessibility**: larger tap targets, colorblind-friendly icons (already
  uses distinct emoji per group, not color alone).

See the open questions in my message — a couple of small decisions would shape
which direction to build next.
